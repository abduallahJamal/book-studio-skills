# Chapter 3: Bulkheads and Graceful Fallbacks

Shipbuilders stopped building single-hull vessels centuries ago. One hull breach flooded the entire ship. Yet many microservices still share a single HTTP client pool across critical checkout calls and optional "customers also viewed" widgets.

When the recommendation cache stalls for 2000 ms, its slow responses occupy every worker thread in the shared pool. Within seconds, checkout fails too—even though payment processing is completely healthy.

> Benchmark result: Capping retries at 10% and isolating pools kept checkout success at 99.4% while the recommendation cache was offline.

## Comparing Failure Containment Strategies

| Strategy | Implementation Cost | Blast Radius Reduction | Typical Latency Overhead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shared Pool (None) | Minimal | 0% (total contagion) | 0 ms until saturation |
| Per-Dependency Pool | Low (config only) | High (thread isolation) | < 1 ms |
| Static Cache Fallback | Medium | Very High (graceful UI) | 2 ms cache lookup |
| Circuit Breaker + Shed | Medium | Extreme (protects DB) | < 1 ms counter check |

Partitioning thread and connection pools by dependency criticality costs twenty lines of configuration. In our load benchmark, pairing isolated pools with a 10% retry cap kept primary checkout success at 99.4% even while the recommendation tier failed completely.
